# Competitor Reframe Protocol — Script Forge Reference

When source material is a competitor's video, article, or content, use this protocol to produce your brand's version.

## Step 1: Identify what the competitor said

Summarize the core argument in one sentence. What is the single insight or framework the competitor is teaching?

## Step 2: Identify what the competitor left out

This is almost always the actionable method. Competitor content tends to tell you WHAT to think. Your content tells your audience WHAT TO DO.

Common gaps in competitor content:
- Concept without method (they explained the model, never showed how to implement it)
- Enterprise framing without SMB specificity (their advice assumes a 500-person company, not a 30-person company)
- Tool recommendation without build guidance (they named the tool, never showed the configuration)
- Theory without math (they claimed ROI without showing the calculation)
- Hype without operational grounding (they described what's possible without showing what to do Monday)

## Step 3: Identify the audience gap

The competitor is probably talking to one of: developers, practitioners, enterprise leaders, or the general curious public.

Define your ideal viewer and the specific situation they are in. The same topic, reframed for your audience, hits differently. The question is not "what does this technology do?" but "what does someone in your viewer's exact role do with this on Tuesday?"

## Step 4: Write your version

The competitor's video becomes the setup. Your version delivers the method.

The viewer who watched both gets more from your version because you include:
- The specific first step your viewer takes Monday
- The build on screen (not a slide deck, a live build)
- The time investment ("45 minutes, not a 6-month project")
- The lead magnet that lets the viewer run it themselves

## Step 5: Never name the competitor

The reframe is implicit, not explicit. You do not say "unlike [Creator], I actually show you how." You just show how. The contrast is obvious without being stated.

Naming a competitor:
- Looks petty
- Invites comparison on the competitor's terms
- Signals that you are reacting, not leading
- Violates the colleague voice (colleagues don't call out other colleagues by name in public)

The only exception: when directly rebutting a specific claim that is provably wrong and naming the source is necessary for intellectual honesty. This is rare and should be flagged for the creator's explicit approval.
