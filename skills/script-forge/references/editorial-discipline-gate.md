# Editorial Discipline Gate

The full authority is your unified voice and editorial guide in project docs. This bundled copy makes the rule fire even when that doc is not loaded. It governs what gets said about the creator. The voice rules (how every sentence reads) live in the same unified guide.

## The principle

Honest is not the same as useful. The creator has real shortcomings, all sitting in the source material a session reads. None of them belong in front of a client or prospect, no matter how earned the lesson sounds. The job is to translate source into content that serves the business, which takes editorial judgment about what to include, reframe, and leave out.

## The brand-position test

Before producing or clearing any client-facing piece, ask:

> If a prospect the creator has never met consumed this finished piece, what would they believe about them?

"They know their stuff and could solve a problem like mine" passes. "Their own systems break / they almost shipped something bad / they are overwhelmed / they cannot finish what they start" fails. The test is the brand position the prospect walks away with, not the literal accuracy and not whether the lesson has a forward-pointing fix.

## Personal patterns are never content

The creator's personal working patterns (e.g., attention challenges, task-switching, over-planning, slow time estimates, products built and never shipped, or other known anti-patterns) are not content. Do not reframe them into vulnerability. Leave them out. These stay private unless the creator explicitly asks for that exact frame.

## The reframe move

When a piece fails the test but the lesson is genuinely valuable, move the creator out of the failure seat:

1. **Observation.** "Here is a pattern I keep seeing in businesses like yours." The creator noticed it, they are not the case study.
2. **Methodology.** "Here is a check every practitioner should run." The lesson becomes a how-to. The creator is the teacher.
3. **Industry pattern.** "Most operators hit this at month four." The lesson generalizes. The creator is the diagnostician.

Subject to observer. Patient to physician. Failure to teacher. The truth survives, the brand position improves. If no reframe gets the creator out of the failure seat, kill the piece.

## Hard rules

1. The creator is never the failure case in client-facing content. Observer, teacher, diagnostician only.
2. No piece leads with their mistakes or shortcomings, even with a forward-pointing fix.
3. No content admitting inefficiency in client work.
4. Personal anti-pattern beats stay private unless the creator explicitly asks.
5. Vulnerability is not a free pass. It must clear "would a prospect want to hire them more, or less." If less, kill it.
6. No framing of the creator as someone who needs help, is overwhelmed, or is behind.

## Rationalizations to ignore

- "They told me this in a chat." A working session is private. Comfort is not consent to publish.
- "The source doc lists this as content-grade." Raw material is not editorial approval. Run the test anyway.
- "Vulnerability builds trust." True in general, false for this buyer. Buyers of professional services want a problem solved, not a person to manage.
- "They have mentioned this personal challenge before." They make those calls in real time with framing control. Default to exclusion, let them opt in.

## Voice

Every produced line also follows the unified voice rules: no em-dashes, no semicolons, no exclamation points, no emojis, no banned words, vary sentence length, open on a scene, take a position, read it back out loud before it ships. The canonical banned-word list lives in the unified guide. Where a skill's own voice list differs from the unified guide, the unified guide wins.
