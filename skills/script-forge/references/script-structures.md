# Script Structures — Script Forge Reference

## Long-Form YouTube Script (8-12 min, 1800-2700 words)

```
HOOK (0:00-0:30, ~100 words)
  Archetype selected and named.
  Curiosity gap: tension the viewer needs to keep watching to resolve.
  Promise-payoff: FRAMEWORK delivers on what hook implies.
  No tools mentioned. The problem comes first.
  [DELIVERY: slow, deliberate, eye contact]

CONTEXT (0:30-2:00, ~300 words)
  Frame the problem. Name the viewer's specific situation.
  One humor move lives here.
  Re-hook at CONTEXT-to-FRAMEWORK transition (new curiosity loop).
  [DELIVERY: conversational pace]

FRAMEWORK (2:00-8:00, ~1200 words, 50-60% of total)
  The method. This is what the viewer came for.
  Screen share segments: [SCREEN SHARE] and [CAMERA]
  Two humor moves live here.
  Sections UNEVEN in length (not democratic).
  Re-hook at FRAMEWORK-to-WTODN transition.
  [DELIVERY: teaching pace, slow on key points, speed up on examples]

WHAT TO DO NEXT (8:00-10:00, ~300 words)
  Specific first move with a verb.
  Time investment stated ("45 minutes" / "30 minutes").
  Success criterion defined (what tells the viewer it worked).
  One humor move may live here.
  [DELIVERY: direct, no hedging]

CLOSE (10:00-11:00, ~100 words)
  CTA is tool-first and optional. Most closes have NO CTA.
  Add one only when the video connects to a specific tool from your
  official collection (roughly 1 in 3-4 pieces). When it does: name
  the tool and give its direct URL (use a pre-created short link from
  your link management system). The viewer uses the tool and sees full
  results with no email gate; email is captured later ON the tool page,
  in exchange for an expansion.
  Do NOT route to a community platform as the primary CTA.
  Do NOT invent a lead-magnet name.
  Do NOT use a bare site link.
  If no official tool fits, set cta: no and close with no CTA at all.
  No pitch. No "book a call." No "subscribe." No email-capture gate
  in front of the tool.
  [DELIVERY: casual, colleague mentioning something]
```

### Hook Archetypes

| Archetype | Pattern | Best for |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Micro-Story | Brief anecdote illustrating the problem. Max 4 sentences. | Real engagement stories, deal-gone-wrong scenarios |
| Diagnostic | Name the viewer's situation, imply they don't know what to do. | Operational gaps the viewer recognizes immediately |
| Counterintuitive | State something contradicting the viewer's assumption. | Industry rebuttals, surprising findings |
| Specific Number | Lead with a concrete, surprising data point. | Cost calculations, ROI math, time audits |

### Hook Engineering Checks

- Curiosity gap: does the hook open tension?
- Promise-payoff alignment: does FRAMEWORK deliver on what hook implies?
- Re-hooks at CONTEXT-to-FRAMEWORK and FRAMEWORK-to-WTODN transitions

### WTODN Validation (all three required)

1. **First move:** A verb. "Export your calendar." "Pick one team member." "Pull last week's emails."
2. **Time investment:** A number. "45 minutes." "30 minutes." "Two hours for the first build."
3. **Success criterion:** What tells the viewer it worked. "You have a number." "One meeting compressed." "A list your sales director didn't know about."

### Lead Magnet (paired with every long-form)

Default format: execution package.

| Format | Description | When to use |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| Prompt Kit (default) | 2-4 prompts the viewer pastes into their AI tool. Each under 200 words visible. No architecture. | Most topics. The viewer runs prompts against their own data. |
| Skill Kit | Ready-to-install Claude Desktop skill with instruction guide. | When the method requires persistent context or repeated use. |
| Diagnostic Kit | Prompts plus a framework document. Prompts produce diagnostic, framework explains interpretation. | Complex methods with multiple outputs to interpret. |

NOT checklists, scorecards, quizzes, or interactive web app assessments.

## Short-Form YouTube Script (45-90 sec, 110-220 words)

```
HOOK (0:00-0:08)
  Information-dense first frame.
  Names the viewer or their situation.
  Specific number or concrete claim.
  Forward pull (viewer needs to keep watching).
  No logos, no "hey welcome back," no slow build.

EVIDENCE / MECHANISM (0:08-0:55)
  ONE mechanism. Not two. Not three. One.
  Specific details from real experience.
  One humor move may live here.
  Quiet Reframe and Straight-Faced Understatement are safest.

TANGIBLE TAKEAWAY (0:55-1:15)
  Stevenson test: the viewer has homework before the video ends.
  Three patterns:
    - A question to ask someone tomorrow
    - A number to calculate this week
    - A specific search to run this afternoon
  "Tomorrow, do this." Not "Think about this."

CLOSE (1-2 sentences)
  Value-close line that lands the insight.
  No CTA. No service mention. No community link.
  Self-contained. The short stands alone.
```

### Short-Form Rules

- One insight, one mechanism. Multiple insights = split into separate shorts.
- Under 90 seconds at teleprompter pace.
- No service description structure (Anti-Pattern 3).
- AI tools may not appear in the hook. The problem comes first. AI may or may not appear in the mechanism. Many of the strongest shorts don't mention AI at all.
- No hashtags in the script body. Add in post metadata only (0-3 relevant tags).
