# Voice and Humor Rules — Script Forge Reference

> **Offline fallback only.** When the filesystem is reachable, the canonical voice authority is your TOV OS — specifically Module B for banned words and craft, and Module D for hooks/flow. Use this file when those modules are unreachable. Where this file and Module B differ, **Module B wins.**

## Punctuation (any violation blocks)

- Zero em-dashes (— U+2014). The single most identifiable LLM punctuation signature. Replace with periods, commas, or parentheticals.
- Zero semicolons.
- Zero exclamation marks in body text.
- Zero emojis. Zero italics.

## Tier 1 Banned Words (any occurrence blocks)

transform, revolutionize, game-changer, unlock, leverage (as verb), synergy, upskill, AI-powered (as lead descriptor), seamless, cutting-edge, disrupt, paradigm, empower.

## Tier 2 Banned Words (two or more occurrences blocks)

delve, utilize, facilitate, harness, streamline, underscore, bolster, optimize (without metric), spearhead, navigate (metaphor), embark, unpack (metaphor), unravel, elevate, multifaceted, comprehensive, nuanced (unless specific), pivotal, paramount, robust, holistic, innovative, groundbreaking, compelling (unless quoting), bespoke, meticulous, tapestry, beacon, landscape (metaphor), realm, journey (metaphor), ecosystem (unless technical), fabric (metaphor), mosaic, symphony, cornerstone, underpinnings, furthermore, moreover, consequently, additionally, notably, importantly, crucially, "it's worth noting," "it's important to remember," "in today's fast-paced world," "in an increasingly [X] world," "in essence," "in conclusion," "let's dive in," "let's unpack this."

**Substitution rule:** Replace with the word you would say to a friend at a bar. "Utilize" becomes "use." "Facilitate" becomes "help." "Comprehensive" becomes "full" or "complete."

## Banned AI-Tell Phrases

- "Here's what nobody tells you about [X]"
- "But here's the thing"
- "Here's the truth"
- "Let me be clear about something"
- "The reality is"
- "What most people don't realize"
- "Here's a secret"

## Structural AI Tells (banned)

1. **Tricolon addiction:** Do NOT group ideas in threes. Use two, four, or five items. Every list in the script should be checked.
2. **Democratic section lengths:** Sections must be uneven. FRAMEWORK is 50-60% of a long-form script.
3. **Fake specificity:** Real names, real numbers, or don't pretend. No "a leading SaaS company" or "studies show."
4. **Hedged non-positions:** Take a position. Your brand has opinions.
5. **Formulaic signposting:** Cut "In this section we'll explore" and "Let's look at." Start with substance.
6. **Symmetric constructions:** "It's not X. It's Y." allowed ONCE per script maximum. Multiple uses is the most recognizable cadence tell in AI content. Associated with AI-fluent content marketers.

## Additional Voice Rules

**Reader as subject:** 80%+ of sentences in teaching sections use the viewer or their company as subject. The creator appears as evidence, not protagonist. Creator-as-subject under 20%.

**Pub test:** Read every sentence out loud. Choose the word you'd say, not the polished one.

**Anecdotes:** Max four sentences of setup. Must be specific and real. If the anecdote could apply to any practitioner in any field, it's fabricated.

**Verified data only:** No fabricated statistics. Every number must be traceable or framed as direct observation.

## Humor Mechanics

Six compatible mechanics:

1. **Deadpan Catalog** — List what actually happens with neutral accuracy. The humor is in the recognition.
2. **Parenthetical Confession** — Confident claim, then honest parenthetical that undercuts or qualifies.
3. **Quiet Reframe** — State industry belief, then state reality. The gap between them is the joke.
4. **Earned Admission** — Admit something unflattering about the profession or the creator's own experience.
5. **Straight-Faced Understatement** — Describe a significant outcome with insufficient emphasis.
6. **Stealing Thunder** — Name the reader's skepticism before they hold it against the video.

## Humor Rules

- Every move anchored to a specific, real-world observation. If the observation could be replaced with "most companies get this wrong," it isn't earned.
- Never signal the joke ("The irony is..." / "Here's the funny thing...").
- Never stack moves in adjacent sentences.
- Never use elevated vocabulary inside humor moves.
- Annotate each move: `[HUMOR: Mechanic Name — why it's earned]`
- The remove-and-read test: delete the humor. Does the paragraph still work? If not, the humor is load-bearing in the wrong way.
