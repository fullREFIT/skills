# Editorial Discipline Gate

## The principle

Honest is not the same as useful. Behind-the-scenes struggles, mistakes, and personal challenges may be part of the source material a session reads. Most of them do not belong in front of a prospect or client, no matter how earned the lesson sounds. The job is to translate source into content that serves the business, which takes editorial judgment about what to include, reframe, and leave out.

## The brand-position test

Before producing or clearing any client-facing slide, ask:

> If a prospect who has never met the creator consumed this finished piece, what would they believe about them?

"They know their stuff and could solve a problem like mine" passes. "Their own systems break / they almost shipped something bad / they are overwhelmed / they cannot finish what they start" fails. The test is the brand position the prospect walks away with, not the literal accuracy, and not whether the lesson has a forward-pointing fix.

## What fails the test and what to do instead

| What fails | Reframe instead |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Creator is the failure case | Creator is the observer, teacher, or diagnostician |
| "I made this mistake and learned..." | Industry-pattern reframe: "Most teams make this mistake because..." |
| Personal overwhelm or chaos as the setup | Methodology and pattern recognition as the setup |
| Unfinished work as proof of struggle | Finished work as proof of capability |

## Personal patterns are not content

Working habits, attention challenges, personal productivity failures — these are not content. Do not reframe them into relatable vulnerability. Leave them out. The content's job is to demonstrate expertise and build trust, not to perform self-awareness.

## When in doubt

If you are uncertain whether a piece passes or fails, assume it fails. Cut or reframe before shipping. A piece you are uncertain about is not ready.
